About this case

A new project, inspired by real work. Vero ID is a new product inspired by a project I delivered as a service provider, through Gabriel Tech Design, for Serasa Experian: a short, project-specific engagement (about two months) redesigning the liveness and facial validation flows of a KYC product. That work was real, with a real client and a real result. Vero ID itself has no connection with the company: the brand is fictional, the data is simulated and every screen was rebuilt from scratch. The only thing carried over is the relative gain from the original A/B test (about 58% fewer camera denials).

White-label

Every company, its own brand

The customer sees the company, not Vero ID. The Brand Kit turns one color into a full scale and refuses combinations that fail WCAG AA. Pick a company and watch the flow change.

One system, any tenant

The company controls the brand layer. Neutrals and risk colors belong to the system, so a score reads the same everywhere.

Contrast with white 0

One design decision

Explain before you ask

The camera permission is two taps that can end a verification. So the flow spends one screen showing what will happen and which button to tap, right before the system prompt. In the original project, that screen cut camera denials by about 58%.
